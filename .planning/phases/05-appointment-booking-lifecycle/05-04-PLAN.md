<assumption_delta_decision>
**Signal:** the assumption-delta detector fired a `pluralization` signal on the ROADMAP phrase "reschedule an appointment to **another** available slot".

**Noun under question:** the relationship between an `appointments` row and an `availability_slots` row.

**Decision:** no-change.

**Rationale:** "another available slot" describes a substitution, not a multiplicity. D-08 locks the reschedule to updating the existing row's single `slot_id` in place, and D-06 confirms an appointment stays with one doctor; an appointment therefore continues to have exactly one slot at all times, and no plural slot relationship, join table, or slot-history table is introduced this phase (the reschedule audit trail is an explicitly deferred idea in `05-CONTEXT.md`). This checkpoint is advisory and does not block the phase.
</assumption_delta_decision>

<objective>
Let a patient move an existing appointment to another of the same doctor's available slots, as one atomic database operation that never leaves the patient holding nothing. This is the last patient-facing capability of the phase and the one with the sharpest failure mode: the naive implementation — free the old slot, then take the new one — is exactly the anti-pattern D-09 forbids by name, because a third party can claim the freed slot in between and the rescheduling patient ends up with no appointment at all.

Purpose: the reschedule path is where two independent concurrency hazards meet. One is the same slot race that booking already survives; the other is two requests targeting the *same appointment* but different new slots, which locks on slot rows cannot serialise and which leaks a permanently-booked slot with nothing pointing at it. Both are handled inside `reschedule_appointment()` by statement ordering and an appointment-row lock, and both are only real once a test proves them under concurrency rather than by reading the SQL.

Output: `PATCH /api/appointments/[id]/reschedule`, the day-grouped reschedule picker dialog and outline row action on `/patient/appointments`, and `tests/e2e/appointment-reschedule.spec.ts` covering the happy path, all six D-10 rejection conditions, the atomicity guarantee under concurrency, and the deadlock translation.

Scope guard: cross-doctor rescheduling is not built and is not a gap — D-06 rules it out as not being a reschedule at all, and the cancel-then-book path that replaces it is already fully shipped by plans 05-01 and 05-02. No reschedule control is added to `/doctor/appointments`. No reschedule-history or audit table is created (D-08).

Flagged assumption (spec-less probe, §A): APPT-08 and APPT-09 both returned `unclassified` from the deterministic edge probe. Their edges are resolved by human-authored decisions — the same-doctor constraint by D-06, the in-place row update by D-08, the atomicity ordering by D-09, and an exhaustive six-condition rejection list by D-10 — with one genuinely open reading carried explicitly in the truths above and repeated here: D-10 does not name doctor activation status among its rejection conditions, while D-19 forbids booking an inactive doctor's slot. This plan applies the same `is_active` guard to the reschedule target for consistency (RESEARCH Open Question 1 and Pitfall 5), and flags it for confirmation at end-of-phase verification rather than treating the silence as permission.
</objective>
